## Complete English article

# Commercial Carpet Tile Backings: Compare PVC, Bitumen, PE and Cushion

Compare the complete tile, then approve its backing, installation system and evidence together. A change underneath a similar-looking face is a new configuration to review.

## Answer First: Choose the Backing for the Project Risk



Choose commercial carpet tile backing by matching one identified construction to the project material requirements, use zones, substrate, fixing system and required evidence. PVC, bitumen, PE and cushion describe different aspects of a tile build; none is a universal traffic, fire, acoustic or sustainability grade. Approve the physical sample and nominated documents before comparing the final commercial offer.

Those are starting points, not universal rules. The exact fiber, pile, total thickness, backing build-up, adhesive and subfloor must be reviewed together. The same backing label can also cover different constructions from different manufacturers, so the quotation should identify the actual product and supporting documents.

| Backing direction | Often considered for | Main checks before approval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PVC-backed carpet tiles | Projects that permit PVC and require a documented complete tile specification | Backing composition and build-up; substrate and fixing compatibility; exact construction documents |
| Bitumen-backed carpet tiles | Projects considering a specified bitumen-backed tile alongside other permitted constructions | Tile stability, total weight, adhesive recommendation, substrate condition, moisture risk and removal expectations |
| PVC-free PE-backed carpet tiles | Projects asking for a documented PVC-free material route or environmental submittal | Exact construction, dimensional stability, fire and VOC documents, installation method and chair-wheel suitability |
| Cushion-backed or attached-cushion systems | Meeting rooms, hospitality areas and spaces where underfoot comfort or acoustic feel is important | Compression behavior, rolling-load suitability, moisture response, adhesive compatibility and replacement method |

The table is a procurement framework, not a substitute for the technical data of the nominated product. Ask the supplier to quote one exact construction and state any assumptions.

**Buyer comparison rule**

Compare backing systems on the same fiber, pile, tile size, installation condition and commercial terms. A cheaper square-metre price is not a like-for-like comparison when the backing changes the installation or replacement plan.
## PVC Backing: Confirm the Exact Tile and Installation System

PVC-backed tile is a construction option to discuss where the project permits PVC. Confirm the complete backing build, thickness, weight, fixing method and compatibility with the substrate. A dark sample backing cannot be identified as PVC from appearance alone, and the material name does not establish fire, emissions or chair-use performance.

If a project specifies a PVC-free construction, state that requirement in the RFQ and ask for documented composition. Compare a proposed bitumen or PE system on the same fiber, pile, tile format and installation conditions. Do not treat PVC-free as an automatic environmental certification, acoustic rating or guarantee of equivalent performance.

Its physical sample, nominated product sheet and written quotation still control approval. The listing does not establish a fire class, caster-use result or compatibility with a particular adhesive.

## What the Backing Changes Under Real Project Conditions



The backing is the interface between the visible carpet surface and the building. It influences how the tile sits on the floor, how the adhesive or tackifier is selected, how the product reacts to moisture and temperature, and how a replacement tile behaves next to the original installation.

| Project factor | Why backing matters | What to ask in the RFQ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rolling chairs and carts | Repeated movement can expose weak stability, edge movement or a construction that is too soft for the use zone. | Is the quoted tile intended for repeated caster or cart traffic? What pile, backing and installation conditions support that use? |
| Concrete and moisture | A slab can look dry while still transmitting moisture. Backing and adhesive assumptions must match the tested site condition. | What moisture limits, primer requirements, adhesive system and subfloor preparation are required? |
| Comfort and acoustics | A cushion layer can change underfoot feel and acoustic behavior, but it may also change compression and replacement expectations. | Is comfort or acoustic performance a project requirement? Which construction and test data support the recommendation? |
| Phased installation | Tiles may be installed in sections while the building remains occupied. Consistent batch, storage and replacement planning become important. | Can the supplier identify the production batch, reserve spare tiles and support later phases with the same specification? |
| Lease make-good or removal | The installation system may leave different residues or require different removal work at handover. | What installation and removal assumptions apply to this substrate and landlord requirement? |
| Technical approval | The project may need fire, VOC, sustainability, maintenance or product data documents tied to the exact construction. | Which documents are available for the quoted backing and product code, and are any limits or test conditions stated? |

## When Bitumen-Backed Carpet Tiles May Be the Better Fit



Bitumen-backed carpet tiles are commonly considered for commercial projects that need a dense modular tile construction, clear replacement planning and a floor that will see regular traffic. They can be a practical starting point for open offices, circulation routes and other areas where the buyer wants a stable tile system rather than a soft residential-style floor covering.

- **Office workstations.** Review the tile with the expected chair-wheel movement, pile profile, maintenance route and furniture layout rather than approving it from a loose color swatch.
- **Circulation zones.** Entrances, corridors and printer routes may need stronger appearance-retention and a spare-stock plan for concentrated wear.
- **Modular replacement.** Keep matching tiles from the approved batch and label cartons by product, color, batch and installation zone.
- **Specification control.** The quotation should state the exact tile size, fiber, pile, backing, total thickness, installation system and documents instead of only saying commercial carpet tile.

**Do not assume**

A bitumen label does not remove the need to check concrete moisture, adhesive compatibility, flatness, batch control or the product's actual traffic recommendation.

## When PVC-Free PE Backing May Be the Better Fit



PVC-free PE-backed carpet tiles are often shortlisted when a project has a PVC-free material preference, a sustainability review or a specific product construction that uses PE as the backing system. The correct procurement question is not whether PE is automatically better. It is whether the nominated construction meets the project's traffic, installation, document and replacement requirements.

- **Confirm the exact material statement.** Ask the supplier to identify the backing material, product code and any relevant recycled-content, VOC or sustainability documentation that applies to the quoted product.
- **Check stability in the real zone.** For rolling chairs, public circulation and phased installation, review dimensional stability, pile construction and installation instructions together.
- **Do not compare on material name alone.** Two PE-backed tiles can differ in pile, total thickness, weight, adhesive requirement, fire documentation and intended use.
- **Review end-of-life assumptions.** If recyclability or take-back is part of the tender, ask what evidence is available for the exact construction and what the local project can realistically document.

For a project that needs a PVC-free route, the supplier should quote the material choice together with the technical data, sample reference, installation method and commercial terms. This gives the consultant or contractor something concrete to approve instead of a general environmental claim.
## When Cushion-Backed Systems Deserve a Closer Look



Cushion-backed or attached-cushion carpet tiles can be useful when underfoot comfort, impact feel or acoustic character is important. They may be considered for meeting rooms, hospitality spaces, quiet offices and selected public interiors. The decision becomes more complicated in zones with heavy rolling loads, frequent furniture movement or uneven substrate conditions.

- **Comfort is not the only requirement.** Ask how the cushion responds to repeated compression and whether the construction is recommended for the actual traffic pattern.
- **Check movement and transitions.** Doorways, ramps, floor boxes and changes in flooring level must be reviewed with the total tile thickness and installation system.
- **Confirm cleaning and drying conditions.** A project with frequent wet cleaning or moisture exposure should follow the exact manufacturer's maintenance and installation guidance.
- **Plan replacement before approval.** A cushioned tile still needs matching spare stock, batch records and a replacement method that the facility team can carry out.

## A Backing Comparison Checklist for Your RFQ



A useful RFQ makes suppliers quote the same construction. Include the questions below before requesting a unit price or approving a sample.

| RFQ field | What the buyer should state | Supplier response needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Project zones | Open office, corridor, meeting room, retail, hotel support area or mixed use | Recommended backing and pile by zone, including any exceptions |
| Traffic | Foot traffic, rolling chairs, carts, furniture movement and concentrated wear areas | Suitability statement for the quoted construction and any limitations |
| Subfloor | Concrete, raised access floor, renovation substrate, moisture condition and flatness status | Preparation, primer, adhesive and installation assumptions |
| Backing | Preferred or required PVC, bitumen, PVC-free PE, cushion configuration or open comparison | Exact backing material, product code, total thickness and technical data |
| Documents | Fire, VOC, sustainability, TDS, maintenance and project approval requirements | Available documents tied to the exact product and construction |
| Samples | Swatch, full tile, mock-up, chair-wheel trial or trial area | Sample route, identification, delivery timing and approval steps |
| Commercial terms | Quantity, MOQ, spare stock, destination, target date, packing and Incoterm | Price basis, lead time, packing plan, quotation validity and assumptions |

## Six Approval Gates Before Changing the Backing



A supplier may propose a different backing during budget review, sampling or order planning. Treat the proposal as a substitution until the project approver accepts it. The following gates help distributors, importers and fit-out contractors resolve the change before a bulk purchase; they are purchasing controls, not a laboratory test or installation approval.

| Gate | Buyer check | Record before release |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Identity | Does the proposal name the exact face fiber, backing, size, color and product reference? | Original and proposed construction on separate lines; unresolved details marked for clarification |
| 2. Material restriction | Does the proposed complete tile meet the project's stated material restrictions? | Composition statement and the consultant's disposition of any deviation |
| 3. Installation | Has the installer reviewed the new backing with the substrate, preparation and fixing proposal? | Nominated installation instructions and installer confirmation of compatibility; any site checks still outstanding |
| 4. Use and evidence | Do the submitted documents cover this configuration and the required zone? | Applicable reports/data sheets and reviewer decision; missing evidence remains an open item |
| 5. Sample and interfaces | Have texture, tile direction, adjoining edges and floor interfaces been reviewed? | Identified sample or agreed mock-up, drawing revision and appearance approval; no inference from a rendering |
| 6. Commercial release | Does the revised offer identify what changes in quantity, packing, MOQ, sample arrangements and timing? | Accepted revision, named approver, exclusions and separately agreed spare-stock plan |

Keep the process focused on the change. If only the backing changes, review the affected construction, installation and evidence assumptions. If face fiber, pile, thickness or format also changes, include those differences explicitly. Approval of the original color does not automatically approve the alternative build.

**Hold the substitution when evidence is incomplete**

A photograph, a generic certificate or a report for a different construction does not close the open requirement. Ask the supplier to identify the limitation and available supporting documents, then obtain the project approver's decision before purchasing or installation.
## Use a Short Deviation Record, Not a Verbal Equivalence Claim



Planning example: an office buyer selected a PP/PVC tile, and a PP/bitumen alternative is proposed. Even if the face pattern looks similar, ask for the nominated specification and installation instructions of the alternative. The supplier explains the product difference; the installer reviews the fixing/substrate implications; the buyer or consultant records the project decision. This is a hypothetical approval workflow, not a delivered VCARPETS case.

| Record field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| Original / alternative | Product references, face fiber, backing, size and color of both proposals |
| Reason / affected zones | Why the change is proposed and which rooms, phases or areas it affects |
| Changed assumptions | Construction details, installation system, interfaces, evidence and maintenance needs |
| Supporting review | Sample references, document revisions, installer comments and outstanding questions |
| Commercial revision | Sales unit, quantity, packing, sample cost/timing and production assumptions |
| Decision | Accept, request information or reject; named approver, date and any stated conditions |

Blank planning fields only. This is not a completed approval, a compliance certificate or permission to substitute a product.

Keep price units visible. Per tile and per square meter are different quoting units; backing substitutions may also alter carton weight, pieces per carton or project allowances. Ask for those details rather than comparing a marketplace headline price. Bulk VCARPETS trade basis is FOB Tianjin Port; sample format, fees, MOQ and timing remain project-specific.
## Buyer FAQ



### Can I identify PVC or bitumen backing from a dark underside photograph?

No. Similar appearance is not a composition statement. Request the nominated product sheet and an identified physical sample; confirm the exact backing in the written quotation.

### Can I keep the same adhesive when changing the backing?

Do not assume that it remains compatible. Have the installer review the exact alternative tile and fixing product with the substrate and preparation requirements. Obtain the applicable instructions before accepting the revised installation plan.

### Is a thicker carpet tile backing always better?

No. Thickness must be matched to traffic, chair movement, transitions, doors, installation method, comfort goals and the subfloor. A thicker construction can create a problem if it does not fit the surrounding floor levels or the project maintenance plan.

### Which backing is best for rolling office chairs?

There is no universal answer. Start with a low-profile commercial pile and a dimensionally stable backing, then confirm the exact product's rolling-load suitability, installation method and maintenance requirements. A chair-wheel trial is more useful than judging from a decorative sample alone.

### Are PVC-free PE-backed tiles automatically more sustainable?

Not automatically. Sustainability should be reviewed through the exact material statement, recycled-content or environmental documents, service life, replacement plan and end-of-life route available for the project. Ask for evidence tied to the quoted construction.

### Can I use the same backing throughout an office?

Sometimes, but a single office may contain workstation lanes, entrances, corridors, meeting rooms and refreshment areas with different risks. A zone-based specification can be more reliable than forcing one construction into every location.

### What should I send to get a useful backing recommendation?

Send the floor plan or area by zone, traffic description, chair or cart use, subfloor condition, target tile size, preferred materials, document requirements, destination, target delivery date and whether you need samples or a trial area.
